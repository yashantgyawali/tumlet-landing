Two years ago, right before I flew out to visit friends in Australia and Malaysia, I remember sitting in a cafe, thinking: what gift do I bring?

I wanted to bring something Nepali that wasn't a cliché. I overthought every option and, at the last minute, grabbed pashmina shawls.

For an Australian summer!

Ugh, sorry Cath.

It felt like a gift for the sake of giving one, with no real thought behind it. I like gifts to feel personal.

Since that trip, whenever I wander around Kathmandu I spot things that would make good souvenirs, the kind I wish I'd found while I was stressing over what to buy. So here's my running list of the best gifts from Nepal. Most come from small Nepali brands that deserve more attention.

## 1. Bluff Momo

_A Nepali card game by Tumlet._

![Bluff Momo](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/bluff-momo.jpg)

My partner and I created this card game so I'm biased 🤓. It's also one of the souvenirs I'm proudest to recommend, because it shows a playful side of Nepali culture that most visitors never get to take home.

If you've ever been to, or even just heard of, Nepal, you've probably heard of momo. Locals love it, and most tourists end up loving it too. We turned that into a card game called **Bluff Momo**.

It's a fast bluffing game. You pretend to be quirky Nepali characters, use their special powers, and try to convince everyone you're telling the truth. It's funny and unpredictable, with plenty of "I knew it!" moments.

The whole game fits in the palm of your hand, so it's easy to pack. Unlike souvenirs that collect dust on a shelf, this one gets pulled out and played with friends, and every game reminds them of Nepal.

#### What it's not great for:

- **Young kids.** The game relies on bluffing and strategy, so we recommend it for ages 13+.
- **Big parties.** Loud music and lots of side conversations make it harder to bluff.

#### Price:

Rs. 1,490

#### Where to buy:

👉 DM us on Instagram [@tumlet.boardgames](https://www.instagram.com/tumlet.boardgames/)

## 2. Titaura

_By [titaurabae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en)._

![Titaura](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/titaura.jpg)

People tend to either love titaura or hate it. Personally, I love it.

Titaura is made from dried fruit mixed with salt, chilli, spices, and other seasonings. The result is sweet, sour, spicy, and savoury all at once, and nothing like the dried fruit you might be used to.

If you've ever tried Hajmola, a few of the flavours have a similar salty, tangy kick, but I think titaura is even better. It makes a good gift for someone who likes trying unusual snacks.

You can buy it from any local market, but I recommend [Titaura Bae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en) because they make their products hygienically, they're neatly packaged for gifting, and they're easy to order online. If you're not sure which flavours to choose, just send them a message on Instagram with your taste preferences. They're usually happy to help.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who doesn't like experimenting with flavours at all.

#### Price:

Rs. 100 and up

#### Where to buy:

👉 DM [Titaura Bae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en) on Instagram to place an order.

## 3. Nepali tea

_By [Nepal Tea Collective](https://www.nepalteacollective.com.np/)._

![Nepali tea](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/nepal-tea.jpg)

Nepal is known for its mountains, but far less for the high-altitude teas grown on its slopes. Tea is a Nepali souvenir that's a little less cliché.

My favourite place to buy tea is **Nepal Tea Collective**. They source organic tea from the hills of eastern Nepal, and their teas have been consistently good. My personal favourites are Heart (Herbal Hibiscus Blend) and Boost (Spearmint Tea Blend), as I usually prefer caffeine-free teas.

Try a few for yourself. They also run tea tastings at places like [Kathmandu Guest House](https://ktmgh.com/kathmandu-guest-house/homepage/) in Thamel, which is a fun way to sample different varieties before deciding.

It's light and easy to bring home, and every cup reminds you of Nepal.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who wants something more permanent. Tea is an experience rather than a keepsake, so if they're hoping for something to display at home, another souvenir might be a better fit.

#### Price:

Around Rs. 1,000 to 2,500 for most gift packs.

#### Where to buy:

👉 Order [online](https://www.nepalteacollective.com.np/) through their website.

## 4. Hemp clothes and accessories

_By Ganesh Hemp._

![Hemp clothes and accessories](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/hemp.jpg)

This is one of my favourite places to recommend because it's somewhere most tourists would never find on their own. Ganesh Hemp sits on the hillside in Budhanilkantha and makes some of the most interesting hemp products I've come across in Nepal.

I visited their factory a while ago, and watching things being made there made me appreciate the products more. You get everything from shirts and blazers to bags, shoes, and backpacks. If you're ordering in bulk (around 6-10 pieces), they can even make custom designs.

You don't see hemp clothes often outside Nepal, so they feel less generic than the usual tourist souvenirs.

If you're buying a gift for someone who likes natural materials or sustainable products, this is something they'll probably appreciate far more than a printed "Kathmandu" T-shirt.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who uses a dryer for everything. I learnt this the hard way: hemp shrinks quite a bit in the dryer, so it's best to air dry it.

#### Price:

Very affordable, ranging from Rs. 500 to 2,000.

#### Where to buy:

👉 [Visit their store and factory in Budhanilkantha](https://share.google/FIGFDIi7J3XjgYuHc)

## 5. Handmade jewelry

_By [@stmaharjan_jewels](https://www.instagram.com/stmaharjan_jewels/)._

![Handmade jewelry](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/jewelry.jpg)

I came across this brand while visiting the farmers' market at Labim Mall one Saturday. I love wearing accessories, especially gold-coloured jewellery, so their designs instantly caught my eye.

I ended up buying a pair of their hoop earrings, and I loved them. They're handmade, well finished, and looked far more premium than their price. That was almost a year and a half ago, and I still find myself checking their new collections from time to time. Somehow, they keep releasing pieces that I want to buy.

If you're buying for someone who loves jewellery, I recommend them. They make handcrafted brass gold-plated and sterling silver pieces that don't feel mass-produced, plus a gemstone collection.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who isn't likely to take care of their jewellery. With a little care, these pieces can last for years, but if they're the type to wear jewellery in the shower or toss everything into one drawer, this might not be the best gift.

#### Price:

Rs. 500 to 2,000 for most pieces

#### Where to buy:

👉 Message them on Instagram [@stmaharjan_jewels](https://www.instagram.com/stmaharjan_jewels/), visit their pop-ups at the Labim Mall farmers' market, or find their pieces at stores like MBSH Nepal in Jhamsikhel.
