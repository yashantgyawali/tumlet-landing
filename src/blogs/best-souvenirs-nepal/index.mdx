Two years ago, right before I flew out to visit friends in Australia and Malaysia, I remember sitting in a cafe, thinking: what gift do I bring?

I wanted to take a touch of Nepal but not a cliché option. But I overthought every option, and at the last minute, just grabbed pashmina shawls.

For an Australian summer!

Ugh, sorry Cath.

It felt like I was giving a gift for the sake of giving one, with no real thought behind it. And that's not how I like to give gifts. I like them to feel personal.

Since that trip, every time I wander around Kathmandu, I notice things that instantly strike me as perfect souvenir gifts. Exactly the kind I wish I'd found back when I was stressing over what to buy. So instead of letting that knowledge sit uselessly in my head, here's my running list of the best gifts from Nepal. Most of them come from small Nepali brands that deserve far more attention than they get.

## 1. Bluff Momo

_A Nepali card game by Tumlet._

![Bluff Momo](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/bluff-momo.jpg)

My partner and I created this card game so I'm definitely biased 🤓. But it's also one of the souvenirs I'm proudest to recommend because it captures a playful side of Nepali culture that most visitors never get to take home.

If you've ever been to, or even just heard of, Nepal, you've probably heard of momo. Locals are obsessed with it, and most tourists become obsessed too. We took that obsession and turned it into a card game called **Bluff Momo**.

It's a fast-paced bluffing card game where you'll pretend to be quirky Nepali characters, use their special powers, and try to convince everyone you're telling the truth. It's funny, unpredictable, and full of those satisfying "I knew it!" moments.

Best of all, the entire game fits in the palm of your hand, making it incredibly easy to pack into your luggage. Unlike souvenirs that end up collecting dust on a shelf, this is one people actually pull out and enjoy with friends. Every game becomes a little reminder of Nepal. 

#### What it's not great for:

- **Young kids.** Bluffing and strategic thinking are at the heart of the game, so it's recommended for ages 13+.
- **Big parties.** Loud music and lots of side conversations make it harder to bluff.

#### Price:

Rs. 1,490

#### Where to buy:

👉 DM us on Instagram [@tumlet.boardgames](https://www.instagram.com/tumlet.boardgames/)

## 2. Titaura

_By [titaurabae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en)._

![Titaura](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/titaura.jpg)

People tend to either love titaura or hate it. Personally, I love it.

Titaura is made from dried fruit mixed with salt, chilli, spices, and other seasonings. The result is a snack that's sweet, sour, spicy, and savoury all at once. It's nothing like the dried fruit you might be used to, which is exactly what makes it so uniquely Nepali.

If you've ever tried Hajmola, a few of the flavours have a similar salty, tangy kick, but personally, I think titaura is even better. If you're buying for someone who enjoys trying unusual snacks from around the world, this makes a brilliant gift.

You can buy it from any local market but I would recommend [Titaura Bae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en) because they make their products hygienically, they're neatly packaged for gifting, and they're easy to order online. If you're not sure which flavours to choose, just send them a message on Instagram with your taste preferences, they're usually happy to help.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who doesn't like experimenting with flavours at all.

#### Price:

Rs. 100 and up

#### Where to buy:

👉 DM [Titaura Bae](https://www.instagram.com/titaurabae/?hl=en) on Instagram to place an order.

## 3. Nepali tea

_By [Nepal Tea Collective](https://www.nepalteacollective.com.np/)._

![Nepali tea](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/nepal-tea.jpg)

Nepal is known for its mountains, but not nearly enough for the incredible high-altitude teas grown on its slopes. If you're looking for a souvenir that's uniquely Nepali and a little less cliché, tea deserves a place on your list.

My favourite place to buy tea is **Nepal Tea Collective**. They source organic tea from the hills of eastern Nepal, and I've found their teas to be consistently excellent. My personal favourites are Heart (Herbal Hibiscus Blend) and Boost (Spearmint Tea Blend), as I usually prefer caffeine-free teas.

That said, I'd encourage you to try a few for yourself. They also run tea tasting experiences at places like [Kathmandu Guest House](https://ktmgh.com/kathmandu-guest-house/homepage/) in Thamel. So, if you're unsure what to buy, it's a fun way to sample different varieties of Nepali tea before deciding.

It's lightweight and one of the easiest gifts to bring home. Every cup becomes a small reminder of Nepal.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who wants something more permanent. Tea is an experience rather than a keepsake, so if they're hoping for something to display at home, another souvenir might be a better fit.

#### Price:

Around Rs. 1,000–2,500 for most gift-worthy packs.

#### Where to buy:

👉 Order [online](https://www.nepalteacollective.com.np/) through their website.

## 4. Hemp clothes and accessories

_By Ganesh Hemp._

![Hemp clothes and accessories](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/hemp.jpg)

This is one of my favourite places to recommend because it's somewhere most tourists would never stumble across on their own. Tucked away on the hillside in Budhanilkantha, Ganesh Hemp makes some of the most interesting hemp products I've come across in Nepal.

I visited their factory a while ago, and seeing everything being made there made me appreciate the products even more. You get everything from shirts and blazers to bags, shoes, and backpacks. If you're ordering in bulk (around 6-10 pieces), they can even make custom designs.

Hemp clothes are something you don't see very often outside Nepal, which makes it feel much more unique than the usual tourist souvenirs.

If you're buying a gift for someone who likes natural materials or sustainable products, this is something they'll probably appreciate far more than a printed "Kathmandu" T-shirt.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who uses a dryer for everything. I learnt this the hard way — hemp shrinks quite a bit in the dryer, so it's best to air dry it.

#### Price:

Very affordable, ranging from Rs. 500 to Rs. 2,000.

#### Where to buy:

👉 [Visit their store and factory in Budhanilkantha](https://share.google/FIGFDIi7J3XjgYuHc)

## 5. Handmade jewelry

_By [@stmaharjan_jewels](https://www.instagram.com/stmaharjan_jewels/)._

![Handmade jewelry](/blogs/best-souvenirs-nepal/jewelry.jpg)

I came across this brand while visiting the farmers' market at Labim Mall one Saturday. I love wearing accessories, especially gold-coloured jewellery, so their designs instantly caught my eye.

I ended up buying a pair of their hoop earrings, and I absolutely loved them. They're handmade, beautifully finished, and looked far more premium than their price suggested. That was almost a year and a half ago, and I still find myself checking their new collections from time to time. Somehow, they keep releasing pieces that I want to buy.

If you're buying for someone who loves jewellery, I'd highly recommend them. They create handcrafted brass gold-plated and sterling silver pieces that feel genuinely unique rather than mass-produced. They also have a beautiful gemstone collection if that's more your style.

#### What it's not great for:

Someone who isn't likely to take care of their jewellery. With a little care, these pieces can last for years, but if they're the type to wear jewellery in the shower or toss everything into one drawer, this might not be the best gift.

#### Price:

Rs. 500–2,000 for most pieces

#### Where to buy:

👉 Message them on Instagram [@stmaharjan_jewels](https://www.instagram.com/stmaharjan_jewels/), visit their pop-ups at the Labim Mall farmers' market, or find their pieces at stores like MBSH Nepal in Jhamsikhel.
